# Placeholders and approximations

Everything in the engine that is **not** a faithful port of the original. These are stand-ins,
shortcuts or fixed answers, kept here so none of them is mistaken for finished work. Remove an
entry when the real behaviour lands, and say in `re/FINDINGS.md` what replaced it.

Kinds:
- **Stand-in**: our own logic doing a job the original does differently.
- **Approximation**: the original's logic, with a detail simplified or inferred.
- **Fixed answer**: a native that always returns the same value.
- **Stub**: a native that is accepted and does nothing.
- **Missing**: not implemented at all. Calls are counted in the "natives still missing" report.

Every entry says what finishing it waits on: a later step of the port, or a large piece of
reverse engineering of its own. Small, self-contained fixes are not left here.

## Waiting on the front end (menus, options, single player)

The launcher's own part is known (FINDINGS 124), and so are the UI managers' page flow, controls and single-player screens (FINDINGS 125–127). `openbg` runs the front end: the main menu, the Quit page, Choose Match (Single Player and Host) and the session's Choose Pilot / Choose Creature selectors are ported, on the GUI scene in OMFBGGUI with its own scripts (buttons, doors). A chosen match's master session starts and its choosers run; the matches themselves wait on sub-sessions (below).

| Replays | Approximation | Recording and playback are ported as the state system does them (FINDINGS 137, 173-176, 183-186, `re/specs/replay-*.md`): the Record Session option, recordable game types (Practice and the single-player matches are Last Man Standing sub-sessions), states taken on `Register*State`, playback rebuilding the session and driving each matched object by its states. A recording is the original's file: a `.DEObjects` whose one object is a `SessionRecord` (contents v5), named "Session Recording, <date>", so original recordings load and ours read in the original; it is kept in `~/Library/Application Support/openbg/Recordings` rather than the data folder (the Replays screen lists both). Manager IDs, state blobs and object references are the original's: session modifiers by name and GUID text, slot modifiers by class, GUID and slot index, creatures by their ID flags (with `SetInstanceNumber`, and a path from the scene, "{No Name}"); owners with an empty ID share one record; only the recorded session's own objects and scene join it, and only managers with the record BOOL. A state carries executor statuses with no count, a creature's position, Euler orientation and its physics' own `PackState` (a fighter's mask, momentum, heading and the rest, FINDINGS 174), and `CData` values (the packed hit effect) as their bytes. A state is stamped with its owner's local time (a tick-based creature's ticks since its creation time, which its clock now starts from) and kept in its manager's history; it is recorded when it ages out (past 40 adds, older than a second behind the newest, at most 22 kept), when its owner dies, or at the end (each record's first manager), repacked then: references resolved at that moment, a fighter's physics through a scratch unpack and pack, its hit bit following the live fighter (FINDINGS 183). In a replay no creature is locally controlled, so its `master` code is skipped while its AI commands run (FINDINGS 175); a restored state runs at once for executors flagged to do so, which the session nodes are and the fighters are not; a replayed creature is looked up by its local time, restored, and re-ticked from the state's tick up to now (at most 100 ticks; a fighter at the next scene update), and a held state of exactly the current tick is taken again in that tick (FINDINGS 184). Going back in time deletes the run-time actors prepared after the new time and makes every tick-based creature hold (no physics or brain) until its clock is back where it was, when it re-sequences; since an identical state is not restored again, replayed fighters stay frozen meanwhile; a pass of more than 2000 ms jumps the clock by the excess and simulates 2000 (FINDINGS 185). Object info +0x84 is written as 0, which is what the original's new record holds (FINDINGS 186). Ours: a state restored again by a creature's own tick happens only in replays (the original also re-takes a live creature's own state registered between its ticks); a non-tick creature's delta tick does not take the held states in its range again; executor statuses are copied as the owner that packed them laid them out, where the original repacks a shared record's states through its first manager's owner (for empty-ID owners of different classes that would misparse); executor `SetTime` on a clock jump is not modelled (their sets restart as in the original). A practice replay follows its recording to within about half a unit once the fighters stand (a few units while they drop in). The VCR panel (V during a replay) is ported from `DE_CVCRControls::ObjUpdate`: pause (the initial state), frame step, 1/2..1/8 and 2X..16X, rewind, the seek slider and Snapshot (saved to the Pictures folder); V opens and closes it through `BVCRControls`' impulse; its speed and observer choices are combo boxes. Auto/Manual Observer hands the observer to its autopilot or to the user as `ObjUpdate` does (FINDINGS 142); the relative mouse is SDL's motion while Manual is on, without capturing the cursor. The Replays screen is a 5-column list with the session info panel as its instruction listing lays it out; Delete and Rename use the standard confirm and text-input dialogs (FINDINGS 159). The info panel shows the game type's picture and one icon per modifier (`DE_CSessionInfoInGameRobot`, FINDINGS 161); a modifier with no icon is a white square, as in the original. |

| `GatherAllObjectsDerivedFrom` order | Approximation | The original's (FINDINGS 192): the transfer agents from the last back, each file's objects in table order. | Main menu theme's sound | Ported (one fit) | Compared with a recording of the shipped game (FINDINGS 133, 135, 154): our mix matches it within about 1 dB per octave up to 3.2 kHz. ## Effects, sound and music

Sound and music are accepted as close enough to the original (2026-10-30): the remaining fits below stay as they are.

| What | Kind | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Music | Ported | Music plays. The game side runs Music.DEObjects' `MusicPlayer` actor and its `BMusicPlayer` behaviour, the `CSpeaker` natives, the session's moods and the mood jumps between segments (`game/music.h`). `app/music_player.cpp` renders the segments with GothicKit dmusic (`third_party/dmusic`), resolving their style and DLS references against the song's `DirectMusicContainer`. Timing and level follow AudioPlayer (FINDINGS 120): the almost-end notification comes DirectMusic's default prepare time (1000 ms; the game never sets it) before a segment and its repeats end, the next segment is played with `DMUS_SEGF_SEGMENTEND` so it starts at the end, and the music plays at −7.6 dB (the speaker volume curve with the global music volume's default of 0.6). dmusic's synthesizer has been replaced by a C port of Microsoft's DirectMusic software synthesizer (the WDK DDKSynth sample, MIT): its envelopes, LFOs, control-point ramps, curves, voice priorities and stealing, DLS1 articulation and 22050 Hz mix, with what the game's own synth (DirectX 9.0b `dmsynth.dll`, on the disc) does beyond it: DLS2 velocity layers, stereo waves, layered note-offs, the DLS2 low-pass filter with CC74 (FINDINGS 133, 135, 150); we then resample linearly to the device rate. The main menu theme has been checked against a recording of the shipped game (see Open questions). Compared with the soundtrack MP3s (omf.niraj.ws; Crystal Cold and Where the Mind Is, FINDINGS 122): tempo matches exactly and tuning is within 5 cents; we have 6-15 dB less treble above 1 kHz, which comes from the game's instrument samples (mostly 11 or 22 kHz) and the 22050 Hz synth, as the MP3s are studio renders rather than recordings of the game. Their mastering (peaks over full scale) also makes them no guide to the in-game level. The style player picks patterns, shapes curves and times band items as dmstyle / dmime do (FINDINGS 151); left out are the chord-rhythm and destination-groove criteria and repeat modes 1-5, which the data does not exercise, and whether a segment-end transition flushes curve resets is not known. A segment without a band track (every song's) plays on its container's first band, applied before it whenever it differs from the last, as AudioPlayer does (FINDINGS 150); the songs had been playing on the theme's leftover instruments. `OPENBG_DEBUG_MUSIC=1` traces it. |
| Sound | Ported (one choice) | Each sound is a speaker sphere updated every frame as `DE_CDirectXSpeakerElement::Update` does (FINDINGS 121): the sphere's intensity, the global SFX volume (1.0) and the speaker's volume, the update-rate accumulator (its length in ms is the whole file's bytes × 1000 / nAvgBytesPerSec, as `DE_CStreamNoise::Length`), the 10000 log10(1 − (1 − v s)²) buffer volume, starting only within the falloff. No Doppler, occlusion, rolloff or voice limit, as in the original (min = max distance 9999999). The pan depends on the player's sound card (FINDINGS 149, `re/specs/directsound.md`): AudioPlayer gives buffers 3D control only when the card reports free hardware 3D buffers, so without them every sound plays centred, and with them the card's own 3D (unknown) or dsound's software 3D applied. Ours uses dsound.dll's WDM software law by default (−5 dB base, tangent law between speakers at ±90°, 1000 log10 of each power share, the 2.5-unit blend); `Preferences\Audio\3D Mode` = 0 (our own registry value) plays everything centred instead. Volumetric sounds play as spheres; looping noises don't occur in the scripts' sound calls. |

## Natives once thought to be OMFBG.exe's

OMFBG.exe registers no natives: it is the launcher (front end and session glue), and its whole
engine import surface has no `DE_CClass` or registration entry point (FINDINGS 123, from the
readable symbol and import tables in `source/omfbg-exe/REPORT.md`; its code stays encrypted and
is not decrypted). What used to be listed here is settled:
