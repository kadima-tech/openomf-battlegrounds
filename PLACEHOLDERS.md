# Placeholders and approximations

Everything in the engine that is **not** a faithful port of the original. These are stand-ins,
shortcuts or fixed answers, kept here so none of them is mistaken for finished work. Remove an
entry when the real behaviour lands, and say in `re/FINDINGS.md` what replaced it.

Kinds:
- **Stand-in**: our own logic doing a job the original does differently.
- **Approximation**: the original's logic, with a detail simplified or inferred.
- **Fixed answer**: a native that always returns the same value.
- **Stub**: a native that is accepted and does nothing.
- **Missing**: not implemented at all. Calls are counted in the "natives still missing" report.

Every entry says what finishing it waits on: a later step of the port, or a large piece of
reverse engineering of its own. Small, self-contained fixes are not left here.

## Waiting on the front end (menus, options, single player)

The launcher's own part is known (FINDINGS 124), and so are the UI managers' page flow, controls and single-player screens (FINDINGS 125–127). `openbg` runs the front end: the main menu, the Quit page, Choose Match (Single Player and Host) and the session's Choose Pilot / Choose Creature selectors are ported, on the GUI scene in OMFBGGUI with its own scripts (buttons, doors). A chosen match's master session starts and its choosers run; the matches themselves wait on sub-sessions (below).

| Replays | Approximation | Recording and playback are ported as the state system does them (FINDINGS 137, 173-176, 183-186, 201-205, `re/specs/replay-*.md`): the Record Session option, recordable game types (Practice and the single-player matches are Last Man Standing sub-sessions), states taken on `Register*State`, playback rebuilding the session and driving each matched object by its states. A recording is the original's file: a `.DEObjects` whose one object is a `SessionRecord` (contents v5), named "Session Recording, <date>", so original recordings load and ours read in the original; it is kept in the settings folder's `Recordings` (`~/Library/Application Support/openbg/Recordings` on macOS, `%APPDATA%\openbg\Recordings` on Windows, `~/.local/share/openbg/Recordings` on Linux) rather than the data folder (the Replays screen lists both). Manager IDs, state blobs and object references are the original's: session modifiers by name and GUID text, slot modifiers by class, GUID and slot index, creatures by their ID flags (with `SetInstanceNumber`, and a path from the scene, "{No Name}"); owners with an empty ID share one record; only the recorded session's own objects and scene join it, and only managers with the record BOOL. A state carries executor statuses with no count, a creature's position, Euler orientation and its physics' own `PackState` (a fighter's mask, momentum, heading and the rest, FINDINGS 174), and `CData` values (the packed hit effect) as their bytes. A state is stamped with its owner's local time (a tick-based creature's ticks since its creation time, which its clock now starts from) and kept in its manager's history; it is recorded when it ages out (past 40 adds, older than a second behind the newest, at most 22 kept), when its owner dies, or at the end (each record's first manager), repacked then: references resolved at that moment, a fighter's physics through a scratch unpack and pack, its hit bit following the live fighter (FINDINGS 183). In a replay no creature is locally controlled, so its `master` code is skipped while its AI commands run (FINDINGS 175); a restored state runs at once for executors flagged to do so, which the session nodes are and the fighters are not; a replayed creature is looked up by its local time, restored, and re-ticked from the state's tick up to now (at most 100 ticks; a fighter at the next scene update), and a held state of exactly the current tick is taken again in that tick (FINDINGS 184). Going back in time deletes the run-time actors prepared after the new time and makes every tick-based creature hold (no physics or brain) until its clock is back where it was, when it re-sequences; since an identical state is not restored again, replayed fighters stay frozen meanwhile; a pass of more than 2000 ms jumps the clock by the excess and simulates 2000 (FINDINGS 185). Object info +0x84 is written as 0, which is what the original's new record holds (FINDINGS 186). A restored creature runs its creature-state-changed hooks at the end of its next tick, as after any change of its behaviour executor's state (FINDINGS 205). Every `Register*State` is kept in its manager's list whether or not the session records, and a creature's tick takes back a state of exactly that tick, live as in a replay: one registered after the creature's last tick (a hit reaction or block entered from the attacker's hit test, `Air.FallFromSpawn` from the spawner, FighterStats' `NotifyOfChange`) is restored at its next and costs it that tick's physics and brain (FINDINGS 201); a re-simulating creature tick leaves its command manager alone. The delta tick of a creature with no tick rate takes back the newest state since its last one, though no shipped creature is delta-ticked (FINDINGS 202); executors have no `SetTime`, so a clock jump only restarts their sets (FINDINGS 203). A replayed session's update fetches each manager's record (`UpdateForReplay`, which only queues the state and flags the manager's fixup) before its scene passes and restores each flagged manager's newest state after it, and a replayed creature's tick fetches its record for that tick before taking back a state of exactly that tick, as `GetState` does (FINDINGS 270). Recordings made by port builds before the per-session clocks (their length is the end over 1000 whatever the speed, and their session nodes' first states are stamped on the shared clock) are moved onto the session's clock at playback, so they end at their real end; the shared clock's time at their session's Genesis is not recorded and is taken as the last of the session nodes' states that follow its Begin less than 2 s apart, which can put their states a little early (0.06 s in a test recording); as those builds simulated the spawn differently, a replayed fighter may fall out and respawn until its first recorded state comes. Ours: executor statuses are copied as the owner that packed them laid them out, where the original repacks a shared record's states through its first manager's owner (for empty-ID owners of different classes that would misparse); a creature with no tick rate and no ID flag 0x80 stamps its states with the scene time, where the original's stay at its +0x1d4, 0 until a state is restored (FINDINGS 204). A practice replay follows its recording to within about half a unit once the fighters stand (a few units while they drop in). The VCR panel (V during a replay) is ported from `DE_CVCRControls::ObjUpdate`: pause (the initial state), frame step, 1/2..1/8 and 2X..16X, rewind, the seek slider and Snapshot (saved to the Pictures folder); V opens and closes it through `BVCRControls`' impulse; its speed and observer choices are combo boxes. Playback runs at the recording's speed (FINDINGS 254): the record keeps the session's modifier GUIDs, so the replayed session's Session Speed modifier sets its scene's scale again, and the record's length is its end time over that scale, in real seconds. The VCR drives the replayed scene's temporal master (which `DE_CStandardSession::Begin` gives every replayed session), so its speeds, frame step (50 ms), rewind and seek are in master time, which the scene's scale then multiplies: 2X at Normal speed runs 2.8 times real time and a seek overshoots by the scale, as in the original. Auto/Manual Observer hands the observer to its autopilot or to the user as `ObjUpdate` does (FINDINGS 142); the relative mouse is SDL's motion while Manual is on, without capturing the cursor. The Replays screen is a 5-column list with the session info panel as its instruction listing lays it out; Delete and Rename use the standard confirm and text-input dialogs (FINDINGS 159). The info panel shows the game type's picture and one icon per modifier (`DE_CSessionInfoInGameRobot`, FINDINGS 161); a modifier with no icon is a white square, as in the original. |

## Waiting on networking (phase 2 done: LAN and direct IP, two players; phase 4's internet play done; phase 3 is full sessions)

Phase 2 (`docs/NETWORK_PLAN.md`) plays a network match between two `openbg` processes: the ENet
transport (`net/enet_link.h`), the join handshake, the client status machine, roster, remote pages,
pre-load and load authorization (`game/netplay.cpp`, `game/netgame.cpp`), Host Game and Join Game
(FINDINGS 413-416). `openbg-net` (phase 1, one process) still builds the same match on both sides
without the status machine. What is still not the original:

| What | Kind | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Transport and handshake | Stand-in (by design) | ENet over one UDP socket instead of the WinSock framing; our own hello (protocol 3, engine version, MD5 of the data files' tables of contents and non-media contents, player name, password) instead of the app manager's messages 2 / 4 / 5 with protocol 10. Every connection is encrypted and authenticated (docs/NETWORK_PLAN.md 7.9); the original had neither. The delay simulator delays sends only, as `Delay Packet Time` did (*inferred*); `Added Variation` is uniform. |
| Session info | Approximation | The join accept carries the master description, its attached modifier descriptions, StartingMatch and Max Clients; the original sends its `DE_CSessionInfo` contents. |
| Roster and slots (0x01, 0x0a) | Approximation | The roster carries each client's ID, name and slot; the slot's own state (0x0a) is not sent: the slot modifiers' replicated states carry the picks. The client sets its own slot locally controlled from it. |
| Client data (0x0b / 0x0c) | Approximation | Sent once a second (the original's interval is not known): a client's name, ping and its kick vote on each client; the server's answer per client (ID, name, ping, administrator level, votes needed). `CClient::Ping` reads them (FINDINGS 450). |
